Tap-to-review stands · South Lake Tahoe

Reviews, in three seconds

A counter stand your guests touch with their phone. It opens your Google review page — already loaded, ready to type. No app, no QR code, no searching for your name. Ready-made artwork or built around your business, programmed by hand and set up at your counter — and on the right side of Google’s and Yelp’s rules.

The problem this solves

Your best guests are the ones you never see again. In season, a large share of the room is visitors — they eat, they leave happy, they drive home, and the review they meant to write dissolves somewhere on the 50. The people who do write reviews unprompted are disproportionately the ones who had a bad night.

Asking works. Asking is also awkward, inconsistent between servers, and easy to forget on a Saturday at eight. And the moment you make the guest search for your name in Google Maps, you have lost most of them: it is a dozen taps of work for something they were only mildly motivated to do.

The whole product is one idea: remove every step between “I’d leave them a review” and the cursor blinking in the review box.

How it works

  1. 01

    The guest taps the stand

    A passive NFC chip sits behind the printed insert. There is no battery, no wi-fi, no power cable and nothing to charge — the phone itself powers the chip for the instant it takes to read it. Every iPhone from the XS (2018) onward and effectively every modern Android reads it with nothing installed and no app open: the guest just holds the phone near the stand.

  2. 02

    A notification opens your review page

    The phone shows a banner; one tap on it and your Google review sheet is on screen — your business name, your star rating, the text box. Not a landing page of mine, not a menu of options. Yours.

  3. 03

    They write it on the spot

    At the table, while the food is still in front of them and the server who took care of them is still in the room. That is the difference between a review you get and a review you don’t.

B — The check-presenter card  3″ × 8″ · laminated · two tap zones

  • Sized to be seen. Check presenters run about 5″ × 9″, so an 8″ card stands well above the pocket and is the first thing the guest sees when the folder opens. A business card disappears inside it.
  • The same two zones as the stand. Google at the top, Yelp at the bottom, far enough apart that a phone can only ever read the one it is held against. The guest gets the same choice they get at the counter — except now it is in their hand, at the most satisfied moment of the visit.
  • One per bill folder — most rooms run ten to twenty, and they are cheap enough to replace when one gets wet.
  • Two finishes. Cream against a dark vinyl folder, navy against a light one — whichever way round your check presenters are, the card reads instantly.

It follows the rules — and most of this category doesn’t

The penalties for review manipulation land on your business profile, not on whoever sold you the device.

No filter screen, ever

Some competitors sell a middle page: “How was your experience?” — happy faces go to Google, unhappy faces go to a private complaint form. That is review gating. Google prohibits it outright and it falls under the FTC rule on review suppression in force since October 2024. These stands open Google directly; whoever taps it writes whatever they want.

No incentives

Nothing is ever offered in exchange for a review — no free dessert, no discount, no raffle entry. Google removes reviews obtained that way and can suspend the business profile that collected them.

Yelp, handled the way Yelp asks

Yelp does not allow businesses to solicit reviews at all, and enforces it with a public Consumer Alert on the listing and a year of lost advertising eligibility. So that end never says “review us” and never opens a review form: it says Find us on Yelp and opens your Yelp page — Yelp’s own recommended wording, from the badges they hand out free. It sends people to your listing; it does not manufacture reviews.
