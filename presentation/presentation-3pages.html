The problem you already know about

Most of the people who walk through your door in July are visitors. They eat once, they say it was great, and they drive home. The review they meant to leave never gets written, because by the time they think of it they are two hours down the 50 and can’t remember your name.

Meanwhile the next visitor is standing in a hotel room searching “dinner near me,” and what decides where they go is the number under your stars. The gap between the people who liked you and the people who said so in public is the whole thing this fixes.

Why it beats the QR code you already tried

A QR code needs a camera app, decent light, a steady hand and a guest who is willing to point a phone at a table tent. A tap needs none of that — the phone is already in their hand and the sign is already at the register. Every piece I build carries a QR code as well, printed small on the same sign, so the handful of older phones are covered too. The tap is what gets used.

Questions I get asked

Does it work on every phone?

Every iPhone from the XR forward, and essentially every Android sold in the last several years — nothing to install on either. For the rest, the same sign carries a small printed QR code.

Does it need wifi or power?

No. The chip has no battery and draws its power from the phone that touches it. The guest’s own phone needs a connection to load the page, which it already has.

Won’t this bring in bad reviews too?

It can, and I won’t pretend otherwise — filtering them out is the exact shortcut that gets businesses penalized. But the unhappy guest was always going to find your page on their own; it is the happy majority that needed the three-second path. Making it easy for everyone moves the average up, because most of your room liked you.

What if we change our name or move?

Text me and I repoint it that day, at no cost. The pieces keep working; only the printed insert needs reprinting, a few dollars.
